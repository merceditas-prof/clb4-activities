Responding to Good and Bad News

CLB 4 - Revision and Pair Practice - approx. 45 minutes - work in pairs

Teacher notes (click to open)

Timing: Warm-up 5-7 min | Language focus 3-5 min | Model conversations 5-7 min | Pair work 20-25 min | Free practice 5 min | Wrap-up 2-3 min.

Pair work: Partner A reads the "Student A" card aloud as if it happened to them. Partner B responds using a target phrase plus a follow-up question. Then switch roles for the next card. Circulate and listen for correct use of "That sounds..." vs "That's...". Students can click "Show a sample response" if they get stuck, but encourage them to try first.

Free practice: Students write or say their own real (or invented) good/bad news and their partner must respond with a phrase not yet used in the lesson.

1. Warm-Up: Good News or Bad News?

Read each phrase. Decide: does it respond to good news or bad news?

2. Language Focus: Full Phrase Bank

Responding to Bad News

  • That sounds really difficult.
  • That sounds really frustrating.
  • That sounds really upsetting.
  • I understand how you feel.
  • Oh no! That's too bad.
  • Oh no! I'm so sorry to hear that.
  • That's a shame.
  • I'm sorry to hear that.
  • That must be hard.
  • That's tough.

Responding to Good News

  • Oh good.
  • Good for you.
  • That's great. Congratulations.
  • That's great. I'm so excited for you.
  • That's wonderful!
  • I'm happy for you.
  • That's fantastic news!
  • Nice one!
Patterns to notice:

That sounds + adjective -> difficult / frustrating / upsetting (used right after hearing the news)

That's + adjective -> great / too bad / wonderful / tough (a short, direct reaction)

Oh no... and Oh good... are informal openers - add them before a fuller response.

After your reaction, ask a follow-up question: "What happened?" / "How do you feel now?" / "What are you going to do?"

3. Model Conversations

Read these conversations with your partner. Notice the target phrases (highlighted).

A: I had my job interview yesterday.

B: How did it go?

A: Not well. They said they need someone with more Canadian experience.

B: Oh no! That's too bad. That sounds really frustrating. What are you going to do now?

A: I'm not sure. Maybe I need to take a course first.

B: I understand how you feel. Something will come up soon.

A: I took my G2 road test this morning.

B: How did it go?

A: I failed. I made a mistake at the four-way stop.

B: Oh no! I'm so sorry to hear that. That sounds really upsetting.

A: I know. I studied so hard for it.

B: Don't worry, you can book another test. You will get it next time.

A: Guess what? I passed my citizenship test!

B: Oh good! That's great. Congratulations! When is the ceremony?

A: Next month. I studied for three months for this.

B: That's wonderful. I'm so excited for you.
